Greetings

Welcome to the First International Biocomputers Conference, or Biocomputers 2004 Seoul, Korea.

During the last half a century, computers have been getting smaller and smaller. The mainframe computers have been replaced by minicomputers which in turn have been substituted for workstations and personal computers. Now, most of the tasks perfomed by these machines are done by notebook computers and even by palmtops, and we expect to see wearable computer devices. If we extrapolate this trend of miniaturization and diversification, it will not really be surprising to see the computers going into the human bodies, making biocomputers, say, for biomedical purposes.

The idea of building computers inspired by biological systems goes back to the early days of digital computers and artificial intelligence, however it is only mid 1990s that the feasibility of practical computations using biomaterials was demonstrated. During the last decade, the research areas of biocomputers have been expanded and advanced rapidly. Thus, for example, it has recently been demonstrated that DNA-based biocomputers can deliver drug-like molecules to treat cancers autonomously and intelligently.

One distinguishing feature of biocomputers is that they can receive the wet genomic data and process them directly without any intermediate process of encoding into and decoding from digital information. This is an enormous advantage over silicon technology when applied to data processing in life science and health care. In the long run, one can think of solving the computational problems addressed currently by the bioinformatics software directly by biocomputer hardware. This trend will be accelerated by the advancement of lab-on-a-chip technology for processing biological materials on a single chip.  

Another important point to note is that the advancement of this emerging technology is possible only through the interdisciplinary collaboration between traditionally-separated research areas and the cooperation between academia and industry. The Biocomputers conference ames to provide a forum for exchanging ideas and providing visions for research and industrialization of biocomputers. The conference program will emphasize the technology, application, and industrial aspects of biocomputers such as in life science, medical diagnosis and therapeutics, drug discovery and development, and agricultural and environmental industry, and biodefense.  

Unlike the usual international conferences, this meeting will strive to have a small number of high-quality oral presentations by international experts and to keep the program very focused on the technology and applications of biocomputers. This year the speakers were invited from China, Japan, and Korea to discuss the recent research activities in these countries. On behalf of the program committee I would like to thank all the speakers who agreed to present their excellent and up-to-date work. Also, I would like to thank Professor Masayuki Yamamura and Professor Zhizhou Zhang for their contributions to the program from Japan and China. Thanks are also due to Professor John Reif who showed great interest in attending this meeting but could not make it this time. I would also take this opportunity to thank Professor Chong Ho Lee for his encouragement and support without which I would have not dared to initiate a new conference series of this international scale.

The idea of this meeting was first discussed with Professors Hagiya and Suyama in February 2004 when I was invited to the University of Tokyo for a talk. As a first gathering, we start small this year, but I hope the program will be interesting enough to excite and benefit the attendees both from academia and from industry, both from the IT world and the BT world. Just as the silicon-based computers have revolutionized the way we do office data processing during the IT age, the biocomputers will revolutionize the way we do laboratory data processing in the BT age. The road ahead might be bumpy and long, but given an equivalent amount of time and efforts invested into the silicon-based computers during the last decades, I am quite sure the carbon-based biocomputers will prevail during the decades ahead. It is my hope that this conference provides a venue for discussing the ideas and results on this line of research and development. Enjoy your conference!
