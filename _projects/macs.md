The MACS project investigated how **cooperative behaviors such as homing and herding** could be automatically evolved for teams of autonomous mobile robots — a foundational project in the lab's robotics research.

## The Problem

How do you program a team of robots to cooperate without explicitly coding the cooperation? MACS answered: don't program it — evolve it.

## Technical Approach

- **Genetic programming** to evolve neural network controllers for individual robots
- Homogeneous robot team: all robots share the same evolved control policy
- **Multi-objective fitness**: balancing individual task completion with team-level cooperative behavior
- **Emergent cooperation**: group behaviors (herding, formation-keeping) emerge from individually evolved policies

## Key Publication

- Zhang & Hong (1997). "Multinet Neural Architecture for Evolving Collective Robotic Intelligence." *ICONIP 1997.* Combining recurrent networks with self-organizing maps for goal-directed group motion.

## Legacy

MACS established the lab's early commitment to **evolutionary approaches to robot intelligence**, a thread that continued through EOS, RoboMotion, CogHRI, and the modern Embodied AI and RoboCup projects.
